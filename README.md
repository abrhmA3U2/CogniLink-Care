## CogniLink-Care
CogniLink-Care is an open-source program designed for healthcare professionals and care facilities. Aiming at addressing cognitive decline and conditions like Alzheimer's type dementia—for which definitive curative treatments do not yet exist—this program integrates biochemical dietary approaches and evidence-based multitasking exercises into a strictly timed framework to optimize the secretion and maintenance of acetylcholine in the brain.
------------------------------
## 1. Core Concepts and Scientific Evidence
This program integrates three distinct elements into a chronological timeline to foster synergistic effects:

   1. Pre-emptive Biochemical Supply (Dietary Approach)
   Oral intake of choline and lecithin (phosphatidylcholine) requires approximately 45 to 60 minutes to be digested, absorbed, cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB), and synthesize into acetylcholine. To align with this pharmacodynamic peak, specialized "brain-activity snacks/drinks" are provided exactly one hour before the exercise session begins.
   2. 40 Hz (Gamma Band) Entrainment (Physical Stimulation Approach)
   Based on clinical trial data from institutions such as the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), 40 Hz flickering light and modulated auditory stimuli are introduced to stimulate microglial activity and enhance neural network synchronization.
   3. Cognicise (Dual-Task Exercise Approach)
   Based on validation data from institutions such as the National Center for Geriatrics and Gerontology (NCGG), simultaneous execution of physical movement and cognitive tasks is implemented to stimulate synaptic plasticity and network reconfiguration in surviving neurons.

------------------------------
## 2. Operational Timeline (45-Minute Session Specification)
To prevent the depletion of neurotransmitters (cerebral fatigue) caused by high-load cognitive tasks, this program employs a strict interval design.

[13:00] Serving of "Brain-Activity Snack/Drink" after lunch (Nutrient intake & absorption phase)
   │  (* Allow approximately 45–60 minutes for digestion to elevate precursor concentration in the brain)
[14:00] [Phase 1] Activation of 40 Hz BGM + Warm-up (2-color ball-processing task / 10 minutes)
   │

   │
[14:15] [Phase 2] Maximum Load Task (3 to 4-color ball processing with non-dominant hand and inverse-rules / 10 minutes)
   │

   │
[14:30] [Phase 3] Active Prediction Task (Tabletop Petanque or Billiards-style game / 10 minutes)
   │
[14:40] End of Program & Cool-down

## 3. Phase Procedures and Risk Management## A. Screening and Real-Time Evaluation

* Untested Stage (General Community Intake): The program is introduced under neutral terms such as "Active Brain Game Gathering" to avoid inducing psychological stigma. Staff document the frequency of objective indicators, such as word-finding pauses and processing delays, using a standardized check sheet.
* Difficulty Calibration (Maintaining a 70% Success Rate): To mitigate excessive stress-hormone (cortisol) secretion resulting from repeated errors, staff monitor user facial expressions and freeze times. Rules and color counts are modulated in real time to maintain a target success rate of approximately 70%.

## B. Physical Safety Management (Fall and Fracture Prevention)

* Criteria for Transitioning to Standing Game (Phase 3): Limited exclusively to users who pass an objective mobility assessment administered by a Physical Therapist (PT) or qualified professional (e.g., Timed Up and Go Test under 13.5 seconds) and complete pre-exercise stretching.
* Integration of Assistive Technology: During standing tasks, users utilize existing weight-supporting (body-weight support) gait rehabilitation robots or AI-driven smart walking-assist devices. This framework complements staff resources and provides physical restraint against forward balance loss. Users who do not meet the criteria execute the task from a seated position while holding safety handrails.

------------------------------

## Appendices: Detailed Exercise Specifications (English)## 1. [Phase 2] 4-Color Multitasking Ball Protocol## 📌 Physical Setup

* Equipment: 5 color balls each (Red, Blue, Yellow, Green) with a diameter of approx. 6–7 cm, and 1 collection basket.
* Positioning: The user sits at the table (or stands with robotic assistance). The staff member stands on the opposite side of the table (approx. 1.5m to 2m away).

## 📌 4-Level Difficulty Schema## [Level 1: Basic Reaction (2 Colors / Dominant Hand)]

* Hand Restriction: Dominant hand only.
* Red: Deflect/slap away the ball rolled by the staff.
* Blue: Catch the ball and place it into the basket.

## [Level 2: Neuroplastic Trigger (2 Colors / Non-Dominant Hand)]

* Hand Restriction: Strictly Non-dominant hand only.
* Red: Deflect/slap away using the non-dominant hand.
* Blue: Catch and basket using the non-dominant hand.

## [Level 3: Multi-Task Expansion (3 Colors / Non-Dominant Hand)]

* Hand Restriction: Strictly Non-dominant hand only.
* Red: Deflect using the non-dominant hand.
* Blue: Catch and basket using the non-dominant hand.
* Yellow [New]: Catch with the non-dominant hand and throw it back accurately to the staff.

## [Level 4: Stroop Interference (4 Colors / Cognitive Overload)]

* Hand Restriction: Dependent on oral cues.
* Red, Blue, Yellow: Executed with the non-dominant hand as specified in Level 3.
* Green [New / Interference Stimulus]:
* If staff rolls the ball and calls out "Right!" ➡ User must use their Left hand.
   * If staff calls out "Left!" ➡ User must use their Right hand.

## 2. [Phase 3] Tabletop Petanque-Style Game## 📌 Physical Setup

* Equipment: The same color balls used in Phase 2, 1 target ball (white jack ball), and a table (length of 1.8m or more recommended).
* Positioning: The target (white ball) is placed at the far end of the table (approx. 1.5m away).

## 📌 Rule Specifications
Shifts the cognitive gear from passive reaction to an "active prediction task" that requires spatial estimation and force control.

   1. Non-Dominant Hand Delivery: The user rolls the ball across the tabletop using their non-dominant hand.
   2. Distance Prediction (Force Control): The objective is to stop the rolled ball as close to the target (white ball) as possible.
   3. Cognitive Flexibility Load:
   * Odd-numbered attempts: "Get as close to the target as possible."
      * Even-numbered attempts: "Stop the ball exactly on a line 30 cm in front of the target."
   4. Safety in Standing Position: Users performing this task while standing utilize robotic stabilization mechanisms to safely modulate shift-of-gravity during the forward-leaning motion.

## 1. Submission Criteria for Pull Requests
Any proposal seeking to modify or append data to the core "Operational Timeline" or "Exercise Protocols" must fulfill the following requirements:

When proposing a new exercise task or adjustments to physical stimulation, you must provide clear citations (DOIs or URLs) to peer-reviewed literature (e.g., scientific papers indexed in PubMed or validation data from public medical research institutions). * Explicit Risk Management:
Proposals for new exercise protocols must integrate objective assessment criteria intended for physical therapists or qualified professionals, alongside explicit difficulty calibration rules designed to mitigate fall risks and excessive neurological stress (cortisol secretion).

## 2. Review and Merge Process
All submitted proposals undergo the following structured progression before being merged into the official master repository:

   1. Automated Checks: The system verifies formatting and validates link integrity.
   2. Expert Peer Review:
   Members of the "CogniLink-Care Medical Review Committee" (comprising volunteer physicians, physical therapists, and academic researchers) perform a clinical evaluation of the PR.
   3. Open Discussion: Community members engage in objective deliberations regarding scientific validity and operational feasibility within the GitHub Issue or PR thread.
   4. Approval: A modification is merged into the official specification only after receiving a formal approval from the review committee.

## CogniLink-Care: Operational Adjustments & Practical Revisions
This document details the critical adjustments made to the CogniLink-Care framework to eliminate logistical conflicts between theoretical neuroscience and the clinical realities of daily care facility operations.
------------------------------
## 1. Dietary Synchronization: Resolving Care-Staff Scheduling Conflicts

* The Conflict: Serving specialized snacks at 13:00 directly collides with the peak period for continent care (scheduled toileting, diaper changes) and bathing rotations, causing critical staff shortages and elevated physiological urgency prior to exercise.
* The Revision (Implementation): Eliminate the 13:00 snack phase. Instead, integrate the necessary concentration of soy/egg lecithin, choline, and essential B vitamins directly into the facility's standard lunch menu served between 11:30 and 12:00 (e.g., standardized inclusion of eggs, tofu, natto, or fortified soups).
* The Logistical Benefit: This allows an ideal 2-hour window for digestion, absorption, and blood-brain barrier (BBB) crossing. By 14:00, when standard facility schedules naturally transition into recreational/exercise periods and staff availability stabilizes, user precursor concentrations are optimized for maximum secretion during the session.

------------------------------
## 2. Transparent Progress Reports: Mitigating User Psychological Stigma

* The Conflict: Conducting "covert" cognitive tracking under the guise of an ordinary game and suddenly proposing a high-cost (70,000 JPY) blood test creates a sense of surveillance, potentially fracturing the trust between users, families, and facility staff.
* The Revision (Implementation): Abandon covert tracking. From day one, transparently introduce the gathering as a "Collaborative Active Brain & Longevity Check Club," where participants openly visualize and support each other's brain health metrics, akin to a routine physical check-up.
* The Logistical Benefit: Individual score sheets are formalized into a weekly "Brain Vitality Progress Report" handed openly to users and their families. When a consistent downward trend (below the 70% success threshold) is verified, staff can seamlessly guide the family toward professional medical consultation as a natural, expected progression within a trusted framework.

------------------------------
## 3. Dual-Tier Environment Modes: Overcoming Financial & Robotic Disparities

* The Conflict: Mandating high-cost assistive gait robots limits the utilization of this open-source program exclusively to premium, well-funded institutions, directly violating the democratic principle of open-source accessibility.
* The Revision (Implementation): Enforce a strict [Dual-Tier Branch Protocol] based on a facility's available infrastructure:

## [Mode A: High-Tech Infrastructure]
Utilize weight-supporting gait rehabilitation robotics or AI-driven smart walking-assist devices during Phase 3 (Standing Petanque) to automate stability control and minimize staff-allocation requirements.
## [Mode B: Low-Tech / Resource-Constrained Infrastructure]
For community centers or facilities without robotic access, implement the following strict mechanical layout:

   1. Position the exercise tables securely against structural walls equipped with commercial handrails on the user's dominant side.
   2. Mandate a two-staff containment protocol: Staff Member 1 serves as the game operator, while Staff Member 2 is positioned directly behind the user, providing light manual stability hold at the pelvis during the forward-leaning motion.
   3. If structural handrails are unavailable, the task must be performed entirely from a secured, weighted armchair.
